## Q1. Explain how the characteristics of social media can be utilized by a retail company to understand customer preferences.

1\. User Generated Content (UGC): Customers create posts, reviews, comments, and photos. The company can analyze these to understand product satisfaction, identify complaints, and discover popular products.

2\. Interactivity: Likes, comments, shares, and reactions show customer responses. The company can measure engagement and collect immediate feedback.

3\. Connectivity and Community Formation: Users connect through groups, followers, and communities. Analyzing these connections helps identify customer interests and understand how product opinions spread.

9\. Massive Data Generation: Social media generates large amounts of structured and unstructured data. Sentiment analysis and trend analysis help identify customer satisfaction, complaints, and emerging trends.

10\. Global Connectivity: Customers from different geographical locations can share their opinions. The company can compare regional preferences and plan suitable marketing strategies.

### Conclusion

The characteristics of social media help a retail company understand customer preferences, measure satisfaction, identify popular products, improve customer service, and develop effective marketing strategies.

## Q2. Explain how the seven layers of Social Media Analytics can be applied systematically by an organization.

### Introduction

Social Media Analytics converts raw social media data into meaningful business insights. The seven layer framework provides a systematic process for collecting, processing, analyzing, and using social media information for decision making.

### Seven Layers of Social Media Analytics

1\. Social Media Data Sources: This layer identifies where the data originates, such as Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, Reddit, and review platforms. Data includes posts, comments, images, videos, likes, shares, and network information.

Application: A company identifies the platforms where customers discuss its products.

2\. Data Collection: Relevant data is collected using APIs, web crawling, social listening tools, and surveys. Data collection may include both historical and real time information.

Application: The company collects brand mentions, reviews, comments, and engagement data.

3\. Data Storage: The collected data is stored using relational databases, NoSQL databases, cloud storage, or data lakes. Storage must support scalability, security, and quick retrieval.

Application: The company stores customer comments and reviews for future analysis.

4\. Data Processing: Raw data is cleaned and prepared by removing duplicates, spam, and errors. Activities include text normalization, tokenization, stop word removal, stemming, and lemmatization.

Application: The company removes irrelevant comments before analyzing customer sentiment.

5\. Analytics and Data Mining: This layer extracts patterns, trends, and useful information using techniques such as sentiment analysis, text mining, network analysis, topic modeling, and trend analysis.

The four types of analytics are descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, and prescriptive analytics.

Application: The company discovers that customers like product quality but complain about delivery delays.

6\. Visualization and Reporting: Results are presented through bar charts, line graphs, heat maps, word clouds, and dashboards. Tools such as Power BI and Tableau help managers understand findings.

Application: A dashboard displays engagement, customer sentiment, and campaign performance.

7\. Business Decision Making: Insights are converted into actions to improve marketing, customer service, product development, and strategic planning.

Application: The company improves delivery services and modifies its marketing campaigns based on customer feedback.

### Conclusion

The seven layer framework transforms raw social media data into actionable insights. Each layer depends on the previous one, and effective implementation helps organizations understand customers, improve performance, and make evidence based decisions.

Social Media Analytics helps organizations understand customer opinions and behavior. However, social media data may contain irrelevant information, fake accounts, duplicate posts, and complaints in multiple languages. These issues must be addressed to ensure accurate analysis.

Challenge: Data may contain advertisements, spam, unrelated comments, and unnecessary information.

Solutions:

1. Use data cleaning to remove irrelevant posts and spam.
2. Validate data to ensure that it is relevant and reliable.
3. Correct errors and handle missing values where possible.

Example: A company removes unrelated advertisements before analyzing customer complaints about its products.

### 2. Fake Accounts and Artificial Engagement

Challenge: Fake accounts and bots can generate artificial engagement and misleading opinions.

Solutions:

1. Account behavior analysis identifies suspicious activities.
2. Posting frequency analysis detects unusual posting patterns.
3. Network analysis examines relationships between accounts.
4. Machine learning models help identify suspicious or automated activity.

Identified bot generated posts can then be filtered before sentiment analysis.

Example: The company filters thousands of identical automated messages to obtain a more accurate picture of genuine customer opinions.

### 3. Duplicate Posts and Records

Challenge: The same post or review may be collected multiple times, producing inaccurate analytical results.

Solutions:

1. Identify and remove duplicate records.
2. Standardize data formats, timestamps, and user identifiers.
3. Validate data while combining information from different platforms.

Example: If a review is collected twice, one duplicate is removed so that it does not influence sentiment results more than once.

### 4. Multilingual Customer Complaints

Challenge: Customers may use different languages, dialects, slang, abbreviations, and context dependent expressions.

Solutions:

1. Use Natural Language Processing (NLP) to process customer comments.
2. Translate content when necessary.
3. Apply text preprocessing techniques such as tokenization, stop word removal, stemming, and lemmatization.
4. Use sentiment analysis to classify opinions as positive, negative, or neutral.
5. Consider context, sarcasm, emojis, and slang while interpreting opinions.

Example: A company analyzes complaints written in English, Hindi, and Marathi to identify common issues related to product quality and service delays.

### Conclusion

A company can improve Social Media Analytics by cleaning irrelevant data, detecting fake accounts, removing duplicates, and using NLP and translation for multilingual feedback. These measures improve data quality and produce more reliable insights for business decisions.

## Q1. Explain how link farming can be identified using social network structure and analytics techniques.

### Introduction

Link farming is a form of link spam in which a group of webpages or accounts deliberately creates artificial links to increase the apparent popularity, authority, or importance of a target page.

A social network can be represented as a graph \\(G=(V,E)\\), where \\(V\\) represents nodes such as users or webpages and \\(E\\) represents relationships such as links or follows.

### Techniques to Identify Link Farming

1\. Degree Distribution Analysis: Degree represents the number of connections associated with a node. In degree measures incoming links, while out degree measures outgoing links. An unusually large number of incoming links to a target may indicate artificial linking.

2\. Link Density Analysis: Link density measures the concentration of connections within a network. An unusually interconnected group of accounts may indicate a link farm.

3\. Reciprocal Link Analysis: The system examines whether accounts repeatedly link to one another to artificially increase their importance.

4\. Clustering and Community Analysis: Link farms may form tightly connected groups of suspicious accounts. Clustering and community structure analysis help identify these groups.

5\. Concentration of Links: The system identifies pages receiving unusually high numbers of links from suspicious accounts.

### Spam Detection Techniques

6\. Content Based Detection: Examines page titles, word counts, anchor text, keywords, visible content, and content similarity to identify suspicious pages.

7\. Graph Based Detection: Examines who links to whom, the trustworthiness of linking pages, and unusual network relationships.

8\. TrustRank: Starts with trusted pages and propagates trust through the link graph. Pages receiving insufficient trust can be investigated as potential spam.

9\. Harmonic Ranking: Uses known good or bad nodes as an anchor set and propagates labels through the network to identify suspicious pages.

### Conclusion

Link farming can be detected by analyzing unusual connections, reciprocal links, dense clusters, and suspicious incoming links. Combining content analysis, graph based detection, TrustRank, and Harmonic Ranking helps identify potentially artificial linking activities.

## Q2. Explain how spam detection techniques can identify and control repetitive comments and promotional posts from newly created accounts.

### Introduction

Spam detection is an important application of Social Network Analytics. Spammers publish repetitive or unwanted content to increase visibility and create artificial popularity. A platform can detect such activity by analyzing content, account behaviour, and network relationships.

### 1. Content Based Spam Detection

This technique examines the content of posts and comments.

The platform can analyze repeated keywords, text characteristics, promotional expressions, and content similarity.

Application: If several newly created accounts publish identical promotional messages, the platform can flag the content as potentially suspicious.

### 2. Graph Based Spam Detection

The platform represents accounts as nodes and their relationships as edges. It examines unusual connections, reciprocal links, tightly connected groups, and suspicious network structures.

Application: Accounts repeatedly interacting with one another or promoting the same page may form a suspicious group.

### 3. Behavioural Analysis

The platform examines the following indicators:

1. Extremely high posting frequency.
2. Repetitive messages and similar replies.
3. Unusual follow and following activity.
4. Sudden bursts of activity.
5. Repeated hashtags and unusual network patterns.

Combining these indicators helps identify suspicious behaviour.

### 4. TrustRank

TrustRank begins with known trustworthy pages and propagates trust through the link graph. Pages receiving insufficient trust can be investigated for possible spam activity.

### 5. Harmonic Ranking

Harmonic Ranking uses known good or bad accounts as anchor nodes. It propagates labels or information through the network to help identify other potentially suspicious accounts.

### 6. Account Classification

The platform can classify accounts into three categories:

1. Normal User
2. Potential Spam
3. Bot like Account

Classification is based on behavioural and network features. Automated results should be interpreted carefully because false positives are possible.

### 7. Controlling Spam

The platform should identify repetitive content, detect suspicious account groups, classify potentially harmful accounts, and combine automated detection with human review.

### Conclusion

Content based detection identifies repetitive messages, while graph based and behavioural analysis identify suspicious relationships and activities. # Q4. Describe the major methods used for measuring and collecting social network data. Explain the importance of each method in social network analysis.

### Introduction

Social Network Analysis studies relationships and interactions between users, accounts, posts, and organizations. A social network is represented as a graph \\(G=(V,E)\\), where \\(V\\) represents nodes and \\(E\\) represents relationships or edges.

### 1. Methods of Collecting Social Network Data

1\. Identifying Nodes and Attributes

Nodes represent entities such as users, posts, blogs, or organizations. Each node is assigned an identifier and relevant attributes.

Importance: It identifies the entities being studied and establishes the foundation for network analysis.

2\. Identifying Relationships and Interactions

Edges represent relationships such as friendships, follows, hyperlinks, citations, and interactions. Each relationship records the source node, destination node, and relationship type.

Importance: It helps identify how users are connected and how information spreads through the network.

3\. Recording Timestamps and Interaction Values

The researcher records timestamps and interaction counts wherever available. Repeated interactions can be represented using edge weights.

Importance: Timestamps help study changes over time, while interaction values measure the intensity of relationships.

### 2. Methods of Representing Network Data

4\. Directed and Undirected Graphs

A directed graph represents relationships with a specific direction, such as following. An undirected graph represents mutual relationships, such as friendship.

Importance: It ensures that the network correctly represents the type of relationship being studied.

5\. Weighted and Unweighted Graphs

An unweighted graph records only whether a relationship exists. A weighted graph records its strength or interaction frequency.

Importance: These graphs help measure both the existence and intensity of relationships.

6\. Unipartite and Bipartite Networks

A unipartite network contains one general type of node, such as users connected to users. A bipartite network contains two different types of nodes, such as users connected to hashtags.

Importance: It helps represent relationships between similar or different types of entities.

7\. Adjacency Matrix

An adjacency matrix represents connections between nodes. In an unweighted graph, 1 indicates an existing edge and 0 indicates no edge. In a weighted graph, entries can contain edge weights.

Importance: It provides a structured representation of relationships between nodes.

### 3. Major Methods of Measuring Social Network Data

8\. Network Size

Network size is measured by the number of nodes and edges.

\\[ N=|V|,\qquad M=|E| \\]

Importance: It indicates the scale of the network and the total number of relationships.

9\. Degree and Weighted Degree

In degree measures incoming connections, while out degree measures outgoing connections. Weighted degree measures the total weight of connections.

Importance: These measures identify highly connected users and the intensity of their interactions.

10\. Connected Components

A connected component is a group of nodes connected through paths. The Giant Connected Component (GCC) is a large connected portion of the network.

Importance: It shows whether the network is well connected or divided into separate groups.

11\. Network Diameter and Effective Diameter

Network diameter is the maximum shortest path distance between two nodes. Effective diameter uses the 90th percentile of reachable pairwise distances.

Importance: These measures indicate how far apart users are and how many network connections may be needed to reach others.

12\. Degree Distribution

Degree distribution examines the number of connections across all nodes. Many real world networks contain numerous nodes with few connections and a small number of highly connected nodes.

Importance: It helps identify important hubs and unusual network patterns.

13\. Community Structure and Modularity

Community structure identifies groups of densely connected nodes. Modularity provides a quantitative measure of community structure.

Importance: It helps identify groups of users with common connections and understand network organization.

14\. Burstiness

Burstiness measures the tendency of user activity to occur in concentrated periods rather than at a constant rate.

Importance: It helps identify sudden increases in interactions and unusual activity patterns.

### 4. Static and Dynamic Analysis

Static analysis examines the network at a particular point in time, including its size, connections, and communities.

Dynamic analysis studies how the network changes over time, including the addition of users, new relationships, changes in connected components, and variations in activity.

Importance: Static analysis provides a snapshot of the network, while dynamic analysis explains its growth and evolution.

### Conclusion

Social network data is collected by identifying nodes, relationships, attributes, timestamps, and interaction values. It is then represented using suitable graph structures and measured through network size, degree, connected components, diameter, degree distribution, community structure, and burstiness.

These methods help researchers understand relationships, measure interaction intensity, identify communities, and analyze the structure and evolution of social networks.

For example, if User A follows User B, the relationship is represented as \\(A \rightarrow B\\).

### 2. In Degree and Out Degree

2. Out degree: Number of outgoing links. It represents the number of accounts a user follows.

A user with high in degree may act as an important information hub.

### 3. Major Aspects of Follower Dynamics

### 4. Network Evolution

1. Growth of nodes and edges: Tracks the increase in users and relationships.
2. Shrinking diameter: Shorter paths may allow information to travel through fewer network connections.
3. Gelling point: Disconnected components merge into a Giant Connected Component (GCC).
4. Densification power law: \\(E(t)\propto N(t)^\beta\\). When \\(\beta>1\\), connections grow faster than the number of users.

## Q7. Explain suitable methods to collect and measure social network data for identifying relationships and interactions among users.

### Introduction

Social Network Analytics studies relationships and interactions between users. A social network is represented as a graph \\(G=(V,E)\\), where \\(V\\) represents nodes and \\(E\\) represents relationships.

### 1. Methods of Collecting Social Network Data

1\. Identify nodes and attributes: Identify users, accounts, or posts and record relevant information such as unique user identifiers and node types.

2\. Identify relationships: Collect relationships such as friendships, following, interactions, and associations. Record the source node, destination node, and relationship type.

3\. Record timestamps and interaction values: Record when interactions occur and how frequently users interact. Repeated interactions can be represented using edge weights.

### 2. Constructing the Network Graph

The process involves collecting records, identifying nodes and relationships, adding timestamps and weights, constructing the graph, and calculating network metrics.

The researcher can use directed graphs for following relationships and undirected graphs for mutual relationships. Weighted graphs record the strength of relationships, while unweighted graphs record only their existence.

### 3. Methods of Measuring Social Network Data

1\. Network size: Measures the number of nodes and edges.

\\[ N=|V|,\qquad M=|E| \\]

Here, \\(N\\) represents the number of users and \\(M\\) represents the number of relationships.

2\. Degree measurement: In degree measures incoming connections, while out degree measures outgoing connections. It helps identify highly connected users.

3\. Weighted degree: Measures the total weight of a users connections. It shows interaction intensity rather than just the number of relationships.

4\. Connected components: Identify groups in which users are connected through paths. The Giant Connected Component represents a large connected part of the network.

5\. Network diameter: Measures the maximum shortest path distance between nodes. Effective diameter uses the 90th percentile of reachable pairwise distances.

6\. Degree distribution: Examines the distribution of connections across all users and helps identify highly connected hubs.

7\. Community structure: Identifies groups with dense internal connections and relatively fewer connections between groups. Modularity measures community structure.

8\. Burstiness: Identifies concentrated periods of user activity, such as sudden increases in interactions.

### 4. Static and Dynamic Network Analysis

Static analysis studies the network at a particular point in time, including its size, connections, communities, and diameter.

Dynamic analysis studies changes over time, such as new users, new relationships, component growth, and changing interaction patterns.

### Conclusion

Researchers should collect user and relationship data, construct a suitable graph, and calculate network metrics. Network size, degree, weighted degree, connected components, diameter, community structure, and burstiness help identify relationships and understand user interactions and network evolution.

## Q8. What is link farming? Explain its working mechanism and impact on the structure and reliability of social networks.

### Introduction

Link farming is a form of link spam in which webpages or accounts deliberately create artificial links to manipulate the apparent popularity, authority, or importance of a target page.

### 1. Working Mechanism of Link Farming

1\. Creating artificial nodes: A spammer creates or controls multiple webpages or accounts.

2\. Creating artificial edges: These accounts generate links pointing towards a target page.

3\. Reinforcing the target: Supporting pages may use reciprocal links, blog links, forum links, comments, and networks of related spam pages.

4\. Manipulating importance: Link analysis algorithms may treat incoming links as signs of authority or popularity. Artificial links attempt to make the target appear more important than it genuinely is.

The network can be represented as:

\\[ E=E\_{\text{genuine}}+E\_{\text{artificial}} \\]

This means artificial links are added to genuine relationships.

### 2. Impact on Network Structure

1\. Unusual degree distribution: A target may receive an abnormally large number of incoming links.

2\. Increased link density: Suspicious accounts may form groups with unusually concentrated connections.

3\. Abnormal reciprocal links: Accounts may repeatedly link to one another to reinforce the target.

4\. Unusual clustering: Link farms may create tightly connected groups of suspicious accounts.

5\. Concentration of links: Many artificial links may point towards the same target page.

### 3. Impact on Network Reliability

1\. Artificial popularity: A page may appear popular without genuine user interest.

2\. Manipulated authority: Artificial links distort the apparent importance and relevance of a page.

3\. Ranking manipulation: Link farms may manipulate ranking and recommendation mechanisms.

4\. Spread of unwanted content: Artificial links can increase the visibility of unwanted or deceptive content.

5\. Misleading analysis: Artificial relationships reduce the reliability of network based popularity measurements.

### 4. Detection Techniques

1. Content based detection: Examines word counts, titles, anchor text, keywords, visible content, and content similarity.
2. Graph based detection: Studies linking relationships, suspicious nodes, link concentration, and network structure.
3. TrustRank: Propagates trust from known trustworthy pages to other pages.
4. Harmonic Ranking: Propagates labels from known good or bad nodes to help identify suspicious pages.

### Conclusion

Link farming artificially increases the importance of a target page by creating coordinated links. It creates abnormal network structures and makes popularity and authority measurements unreliable. Content analysis, graph analysis, TrustRank, and Harmonic Ranking help detect suspicious linking patterns.

1. Web crawling: Automatically explores webpages and collects information.
2. Indexing: Organizes and stores collected information in a database for quick retrieval.

### 2. Directories

Examples: Yahoo Directory, Open Directory, and LookSmart.

1. Website submission: Website owners submit their websites.
2. Human review: An editor examines the submitted website.
3. Directory listing: Approved websites are added to the directory.

Importance: They provide organized website listings based on human reviewed submissions.

### 3. Metasearch Engines

Examples: Metacrawler, Mamma, and Dogpile.

Metasearch engines compile and display results from multiple individual search engines.

Their working mechanism involves:

1. The user submits a search query.
2. The metasearch engine obtains results from multiple search engines.
3. It compiles the results.
4. The combined results are displayed to the user.

Importance: They allow users to access search results from multiple search engines through one query.

### 4. Local and Global Search Engines

Local search engines search only the content of a particular website.

Global search engines search across the web and can also support searches limited to particular websites. Google and Bing are examples of global search engines.

### Conclusion

Crawler based search engines automatically crawl, index, and retrieve webpages. Directories depend on human editors to review website listings. Metasearch engines compile results from several search engines. All three help users find relevant information using different methods.
